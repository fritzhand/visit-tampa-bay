I

Era 1 of 7 · Before 1528

First peoples

4 entries, from about 500 BCE (Manasota people fish and hunt the bay) to about 1500 CE (Tocobaga's capital at Safety Harbor). 4 of them happened at places in this guide you can visit.

8 historic sites from this eraPassage: Temple mounds and the Spanish landings

  1. 500BCE

    No exact date

    Manasota people fish and hunt the bay

    Northeast St. Pete & Weedon Island

    Archaeologists call the people who lived on the central Gulf coast of the Florida peninsula from about 500 BCE the Manasota culture; around 900 CE it developed into the Safety Harbor culture. Weedon Island Preserve says Manasota people hunted and fished the bay and left shell mounds along the coast, and it shows a 39.9-foot pine dugout canoe, about 1,100 years old, that was excavated from its shoreline in 2011.

    You can visit Weedon Island Preserve

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · weedonislandpreserve.org (opens in a new tab) · weedonislandpreserve.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  2. 900CE

    No exact date

    Safety Harbor culture takes shape around the bay

    Safety Harbor

    From about 900 CE until after 1700, people on the central Gulf coast of Florida practiced what archaeologists call the Safety Harbor culture, named for the large platform mound at Safety Harbor. Their towns had temple mounds, plazas and shell middens; the Tocobaga who met the Spanish belonged to this culture.

    You can visit Philippe Park

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  3. 1000CE

    No exact date

    A Tocobaga village on Boca Ciega Bay

    West St. Pete

    People of the Safety Harbor culture lived at the Jungle Prada Site on Boca Ciega Bay in today's west St. Petersburg for about 600 years, from about 1000 to 1600 CE. Most of the mounds that once lined the bay here were used as fill in the 20th century; a plaza mound and a long midden mound survive, partly in the city's Jungle Prada de Narvaez park.

    You can visit Jungle Prada Site

    Source: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  4. 1500CE

    No exact date

    Tocobaga's capital at Safety Harbor

    Safety Harbor

    The Safety Harbor Site in Philippe Park, a flat-topped shell mound about 20 feet high on Old Tampa Bay, was occupied from late prehistoric times into the early Spanish period (about 1500 to 1650). It is thought to be the capital of the Tocobaga, and it was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1964; Pinellas County says the temple mound is the largest remaining mound in the Tampa Bay region.

    You can visit Philippe Park

    Sources: npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · pinellas.gov (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

II

Era 2 of 7 · 1528–1820

Spanish Florida

6 entries, from 1528 (Narváez expedition lands on Boca Ciega Bay) to about 1767 (Cuban fishing ranchos work the 'Coast of Tampa'). 3 of them happened at places in this guide you can visit.

1 historic site from this eraPassage: Temple mounds and the Spanish landings

  1. 1528

    April

    Narváez expedition lands on Boca Ciega Bay

    West St. Pete

    In April 1528 Pánfilo de Narváez landed with 300 men in Boca Ciega Bay, about 15 miles north of the entrance to Tampa Bay, at what is known as the Jungle Prada Site in today's St. Petersburg. On May 1 he split the expedition, sending about 300 men north overland while the ships sailed up the coast; the fleet searched for the land party for nearly a year and never found it.

    You can visit Jungle Prada Site

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · pinellas.gov (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  2. 1539

    De Soto's men find Juan Ortiz

    Apollo Beach & Ruskin

    Juan Ortiz came to Tampa Bay in 1528 on a ship searching for the missing Narváez expedition and was captured by the people of Uzita, a Safety Harbor culture chiefdom on the bay's south shore near the mouth of the Little Manatee River. After escaping to the neighboring chiefdom of Mocoso he lived there until the de Soto expedition found him in 1539; he then served the Spanish as an interpreter.

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  3. 1539

    May

    Hernando de Soto's army lands in Tampa Bay

    Bradenton & Anna Maria Island

    In May 1539 Hernando de Soto's army of soldiers, hired mercenaries, craftsmen and clergy made landfall in Tampa Bay and met fierce resistance from the people defending their homelands. The expedition went on for four years and about 4,000 miles across the Southeast; De Soto National Memorial in Bradenton, created in 1948, commemorates it.

    You can visit De Soto National Memorial

    Sources: nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  4. 1567

    Menéndez visits Tocobaga and leaves a garrison

    Safety Harbor

    In 1567 Pedro Menéndez de Avilés visited the Tocobaga town that was almost certainly the Safety Harbor Site and left about 30 soldiers there. When Spanish boats brought supplies in January 1568 they found the town deserted and the soldiers dead.

    You can visit Philippe Park

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  5. 1763

    Florida becomes British, and the bay is renamed Hillsborough

    Hyde Park & SoHo

    Great Britain acquired Florida in 1763 at the end of the Seven Years' War, and under British rule the bay was renamed Hillsborough Bay for Lord Hillsborough, secretary of state for the colonies; the name survives in the Hillsborough River and County. Native people and Cuban fishermen kept a seasonal camp at the mouth of Spanish Town Creek in today's Hyde Park, smoking mullet to sell in Cuba. Spain regained Florida in 1783.

    Source: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  6. 1767

    No exact date

    Cuban fishing ranchos work the 'Coast of Tampa'

    By the mid-1700s fishermen from Havana sailed each fall and winter to Florida's Gulf coast, whose main fishing ground was the 'Coast of Tampa', including Tampa Bay and Charlotte Harbor; they salted mullet, drum and other fish and hired Native people as guides and crews. A British review of the fishing in 1767-1768 found it no threat, and by the late 1700s year-round camps called ranchos stood between Tampa Bay and Estero Bay, until the U.S. Army removed their residents during the Second Seminole War.

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

III

Era 3 of 7 · 1821–1883

Frontier

19 entries, from about 1821 (Spain hands Florida to the United States) to 1883 (Fort Brooke is decommissioned). 12 of them happened at places in this guide you can visit.

17 historic sites from this eraPassage: A day at Fort De Soto

  1. 1821

    No exact date

    Spain hands Florida to the United States

    The United States acquired Florida from Spain in 1821 for $5 million; Hillsborough County says the area had been under the rule of Spain, France, Great Britain and the United States between 1559 and 1819. One of the first U.S. actions in the new territory was the raid that destroyed Angola, the Maroon settlement on the Manatee River.

    Sources: hcfl.gov (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  2. 1821

    Angola, a maroon community on the Manatee River, is burned

    Bradenton & Anna Maria Island

    Angola was a Maroon community of 600 to 750 people who had escaped slavery, on the Manatee River and Sarasota Bay. In 1821, as the United States took over Florida, Andrew Jackson sent Creek allies to raid it and the settlement was burned; survivors fled, some as far as Andros Island in the Bahamas. Artifacts from the Angola era have been found at Manatee Mineral Springs Park in Bradenton, which the National Park Service added to its Network to Freedom in 2019.

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · hcfl.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  3. 1824

    Jan 22

    Fort Brooke is founded at the mouth of the Hillsborough River

    Downtown Tampa

    Col. George Mercer Brooke arrived at Tampa Bay with James Gadsden and four companies of infantry on Jan. 18, 1824, and the post, first called the camp on the Hillsborough River, was established on Jan. 22. The Tampa Bay History Center calls Fort Brooke the genesis of modern Tampa; the fort stood about where the Tampa Convention Center is today.

    You can visit Tampa Convention Center, Tampa Bay History Center, Cotanchobee Fort Brooke Park

    Sources: tampabayhistorycenter.org (opens in a new tab) · tampa.gov (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  4. 1834

    Jan 25

    Hillsborough County is organized

    Downtown Tampa

    On Jan. 25, 1834, the Legislative Council of the Territory of Florida organized Hillsborough as Florida's 19th county, named for Wills Hill, Lord Hillsborough. At statehood in 1845 it still covered what are now Pinellas, Polk, Manatee, Sarasota and several other counties, with only 836 residents counted, not including soldiers or Native Americans.

    Sources: hcfl.gov (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  5. 1835

    Dec 28

    Dade's command, marching from Fort Brooke, is destroyed

    Downtown Tampa

    In December 1835 Maj. Francis Dade's column of 110 men left Fort Brooke on the military road to Fort King, near today's Ocala, and on Dec. 28 it was ambushed by Seminole warriors; 108 were killed. The battle started the Second Seminole War, which lasted until 1842, and Fort Brooke became a refuge, depot and staging area.

    You can visit Cotanchobee Fort Brooke Park

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  6. 1836

    December

    Fort Foster guards the Hillsborough River crossing

    Plant City

    Col. William S. Foster and 430 men built Fort Foster in 1836 to defend the bridge crossing on the Hillsborough River during the Second Seminole War; it was garrisoned on and off from December 1836 to April 1838. A reproduction stands on the original ground across U.S. 301 from Hillsborough River State Park.

    You can visit Fort Foster State Historic Site Seasonal, Hillsborough River State Park

    Sources: historyandnature.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  7. 1842

    The war ends and settlers take up Pinellas land

    Safety Harbor

    The Second Seminole War ended in 1842, and the federal Armed Occupation Act of that year offered 160 acres to settlers who would bear arms and farm the land. That year Odet Philippe acquired 160 acres at today's Safety Harbor; he is described as the first permanent non-Native settler of the Pinellas peninsula, and his plantation is now Philippe Park, the oldest park in Pinellas County.

    You can visit Philippe Park

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · pinellas.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  8. 1844

    No exact date

    Robert Gamble starts a sugar plantation on the Manatee River

    Bradenton & Anna Maria Island

    Maj. Robert Gamble took up 160 acres under the Armed Occupation Act and arrived on the Manatee River in 1844; his mansion, built about 1845-1850 with tabby walls two feet thick, anchored a sugar plantation that grew to 3,500 acres on which Gamble likely enslaved more than 200 people. Confederate cabinet member Judah P. Benjamin stayed there in May 1865 before fleeing Union forces and sailing to England; the house is now a state historic park in Ellenton.

    You can visit Judah P. Benjamin Confederate Memorial at Gamble Plantation Historic State Park

    Source: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  9. 1848

    September

    The Great Gale of 1848 wrecks Fort Brooke and Egmont Key's new light

    Downtown Tampa

    In late September 1848 the strongest hurricane known to have struck the Tampa Bay area made landfall near today's Clearwater. Only five houses were left standing in Tampa, all damaged, and most of Fort Brooke was destroyed; the surge covered Egmont Key with about six feet of water, damaging the lighthouse built there that year, and cut the inlet at John's Pass.

    You can visit Egmont Key State Park, John's Pass Village & Boardwalk

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  10. 1849

    Jan 18

    Tampa incorporates as a village

    Downtown Tampa

    On Jan. 18, 1849, fourteen men voted unanimously to establish the Village of Tampa with a trustee government, and a week later they elected five trustees. Citizens organized the Town of Tampa on Sept. 10, 1853.

    Source: tampa.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  11. 1850

    No exact date

    Oaklawn, Tampa's first public burial ground, opens

    Downtown Tampa

    Oaklawn Cemetery, Tampa's oldest cemetery and first public burial ground, dates from 1850. With the adjoining Catholic St. Louis Cemetery it covers about three acres downtown and holds Tampa's first mayor, Judge Joseph B. Lancaster, 12 other mayors, a Florida governor and two Florida Supreme Court justices, and it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2017.

    You can visit Oaklawn Cemetery

    Source: tampa.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  12. 1855

    Dec 15

    Tampa receives its first city charter

    Downtown Tampa

    Citizens voted on Sept. 15, 1855 to replace the town government with a city charter, and on Dec. 15, 1855, Gov. Broome signed the act of the Florida Legislature chartering the City of Tampa. The city's modern charter dates from July 15, 1887, when it absorbed the Town of North Tampa.

    Source: tampa.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  13. 1858

    The present Egmont Key lighthouse is lit

    Fort De Soto & Tierra Verde

    After the 1848 hurricane damaged the first Egmont Key lighthouse, rebuilding began in 1857 and the present tower went into service in 1858 with a new Fresnel lens. In the 1850s the island was used to hold Seminole prisoners before they were shipped west to Indian Territory.

    You can visit Egmont Key State Park

    Source: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  14. 1862

    Jun 30

    A Union gunboat shells Confederate Tampa

    Downtown Tampa

    Florida seceded on Jan. 10, 1861, Confederate troops manned Fort Brooke, and the Union Navy blockaded the mouth of Tampa Bay, using Egmont Key as a base. On June 30 and July 1, 1862, the gunboat USS Sagamore demanded the fort's surrender and then shelled the fort and town in the Battle of Tampa; neither side suffered real damage or casualties. A marker in Oaklawn Cemetery shows where a shell fell.

    You can visit Oaklawn Cemetery, Egmont Key State Park

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  15. 1863

    Oct 16

    Union raiders burn blockade runners on the Hillsborough River

    North Tampa

    On Oct. 16, 1863, the Union gunboats Tahoma and Adela began shelling Fort Brooke as a diversion; on Oct. 18 about 100 Union troops landed at Ballast Point, marched 14 miles north and burned James McKay's blockade runners Scottish Chief and Kate Dale on the Hillsborough River near today's ZooTampa at Lowry Park. When Union forces landed again in May 1864, the fort was no longer garrisoned, and they occupied the town briefly.

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  16. 1868

    St. Petersburg's first Black settlers make a homestead

    South St. Pete & Midtown

    John Donaldson and Anna Germain, the first Black settlers in what became St. Petersburg, arrived in 1868 and built a homestead at what is now 18th Avenue South and 31st Street South. The city says that for 20 years the Donaldsons were the only African American family in present-day St. Pete.

    You can visit African American Heritage Trail

    Source: stpete.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  17. 1875

    John C. Williams buys the land that becomes St. Petersburg

    Downtown St. Petersburg

    In 1875 John Constantine Williams of Detroit bought 2,500 acres of waterfront land on the lower Pinellas peninsula. Thirteen years later he traded part of it to Peter Demens so that Demens's Orange Belt Railroad would reach his settlement.

    Source: stpete.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  18. 1876

    Settlers arrive at Spring Bayou, soon named Tarpon Springs

    Tarpon Springs

    A. W. Ormond and his daughter Mary, the first settlers at what is now Tarpon Springs, arrived in 1876 and built a cabin near Spring Bayou; the settlement took the name Tarpon Springs in 1879. In 1880 Hamilton Disston bought four million acres of central west Florida from Florida's governor at 25 cents an acre, opening the area to development.

    You can visit Tarpon Springs Sponge Docks

    Source: spongedocks.net (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  19. 1883

    Fort Brooke is decommissioned

    Downtown Tampa

    The Army decommissioned Fort Brooke in 1883, the year before the railroad arrived. Apart from two cannons recovered from the river and displayed on the University of Tampa campus, no trace of the fort remains above ground; artifacts were recovered from the site in the 1970s and 1980s.

    You can visit Cotanchobee Fort Brooke Park

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · tampabayhistorycenter.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

IV

Era 4 of 7 · 1884–1919

Boomtown

27 entries, from 1884 (Henry Plant's railroad reaches Tampa) to 1914 (The St. Petersburg-Tampa Airboat Line starts scheduled flights). 21 of them happened at places in this guide you can visit.

80 historic sites from this eraPassages: Central Avenue: Black history in downtown Tampa, A day at Fort De Soto, Historic hotels of the bay, Ybor City on foot

  1. 1884

    Henry Plant's railroad reaches Tampa

    Downtown Tampa

    In 1884 Henry B. Plant brought the railroad to Tampa, ending the town's isolation; that year the line also reached the town then called Cork, which incorporated in 1885 as Plant City in his honor. Plant began developing Port Tampa as a deepwater port in 1887, and in 1888 his system finished a nine-mile spur from Tampa to it.

    Sources: plantmuseum.com (opens in a new tab) · plantcitygov.com (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  2. 1885

    Oct 5

    Vicente Martínez Ybor agrees to move his cigar business to Tampa

    Ybor City

    On Oct. 5, 1885, cigar maker Vicente Martínez Ybor contracted with the Tampa Board of Trade to move his hand-rolled cigar business, Ybor & Co., from Key West to Tampa. He bought 40 acres northeast of the town, and work began that October clearing the land for a factory and 50 houses.

    You can visit Ybor City Historic District, V. M. Ybor Cigar Factory (Ybor Factory Building)

    Sources: guides.loc.gov (opens in a new tab) · npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  3. 1886

    March

    Ybor City's first cigar factories open

    Ybor City

    Ignacio Haya's factory went into production first, in March 1886, and Ybor's own factory followed; by the end of 1886 several hundred worker houses stood between today's 6th and 9th avenues and 12th and 14th streets, and more than 3,000 workers had come from Cuba. The 1886 V. M. Ybor factory on 14th Street, the first brick cigar factory built in Tampa, still stands.

    You can visit Ybor City Historic District, V. M. Ybor Cigar Factory (Ybor Factory Building)

    Sources: npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · guides.loc.gov (opens in a new tab) · nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  4. 1887

    No exact date

    The Anclote Key lighthouse is built off Tarpon Springs

    Tarpon Springs

    The Anclote Key Light, a 110-foot cast-iron skeleton tower built to let storm winds and tides blow through, went up on Anclote Key in 1887. It was automated in 1952 and later deactivated; after volunteers had it listed on the National Register in 1999, it was restored and relit on Sept. 13, 2003, and the friends group opens it for climbing on set weekends from October to May.

    You can visit Anclote Key Lighthouse, Anclote Key Preserve State Park

    Sources: anclotecso.org (opens in a new tab) · anclotecso.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  5. 1887

    Tarpon Springs becomes Pinellas's first incorporated city

    Tarpon Springs

    Tarpon Springs incorporated in 1887 with 52 residents, the first incorporated city in what is now Pinellas County. John Cheney, a promoter associated with Hamilton Disston, found that sponges growing in the Gulf could be harvested for money, and in 1890 the Cheney Sponge Company sold almost a million dollars' worth.

    You can visit Tarpon Springs Sponge Docks

    Sources: spongedocks.net (opens in a new tab) · pinellas.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  6. 1887

    June

    Tampa annexes Ybor City

    Ybor City

    Ybor City's rapid growth led Tampa to annex it in June 1887, although the cigar town kept its own identity; by 1890 it had about 6,000 residents, more than the rest of Tampa. The following month, on July 15, 1887, Tampa organized under a new city charter that also absorbed the Town of North Tampa.

    You can visit Ybor City Historic District

    Sources: npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · tampa.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  7. 1888

    The Orange Belt Railway reaches St. Petersburg

    Downtown St. Petersburg

    Peter Demens, an exiled Russian aristocrat, extended his Orange Belt Railroad down the Pinellas peninsula to John C. Williams's settlement in exchange for land, and the first train arrived in 1888 carrying empty freight cars and a single passenger. Legend says the two men flipped a coin to name the town; Demens won and named it for Saint Petersburg, Russia, and Williams named the first hotel the Detroit. Demens built the city's first pier, a railroad pier, in 1889.

    You can visit The St. Pete Pier, Detroit Hotel

    Sources: stpete.org (opens in a new tab) · stpetepier.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  8. 1891

    El Centro Español, Ybor City's first mutual aid society

    Ybor City

    El Centro Español, founded in 1891, was the first of Ybor City's ethnic mutual aid clubs; members paid dues for social privileges and death and injury benefits. Others followed: L'Unione Italiana (1894), the Deutsch-Amerikanischer Club (1901), Centro Asturiano and El Círculo Cubano (1902) and La Unión Martí-Maceo for Afro-Cubans (1904), Centro Español and Centro Asturiano built their own hospitals, and several of the clubs' clubhouses still stand in Ybor City.

    You can visit Ybor City Historic District, El Centro Español de Tampa, Italian Club of Tampa (L'Unione Italiana), Centro Asturiano de Tampa, Cuban Club (El Círculo Cubano de Tampa), La Unión Martí-Maceo

    Sources: npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  9. 1891

    Clear Water Harbor incorporates

    Downtown Clearwater

    The settlement near the Seminole War outpost of Fort Harrison grew after Peter Demens's railroad arrived in 1888, and the town of Clear Water Harbor incorporated in 1891; the name Clearwater came into use in 1906. Dunedin, named by two Scotsmen who applied for its post office, incorporated in 1899.

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  10. 1891

    Feb 5

    Plant opens the Tampa Bay Hotel

    Downtown Tampa

    Henry Plant opened the Tampa Bay Hotel, a winter resort designed in the style of a Moorish palace beside the Hillsborough River, on Feb. 5, 1891, after building it from 1888. It operated as a hotel until 1932; since 1933 the building has been leased to the University of Tampa, and its south wing is the Henry B. Plant Museum.

    You can visit Henry B. Plant Museum

    Sources: plantmuseum.com (opens in a new tab) · nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  11. 1891

    Nov 26

    José Martí rallies Ybor City for Cuban independence

    Ybor City

    On Nov. 26 and 27, 1891, José Martí gave two speeches in Ybor City, 'With All and For All' and 'The New Growth', that set out the goals of the Cuban Revolutionary Party. In 1893 he spoke from the steps of the V. M. Ybor factory to a crowd of more than 10,000, and afterward workers pledged a day's pay a week to the revolution; the site of the home of Ruperto and Paulina Pedroso, Black Cubans who sheltered him when Spanish assassins came looking for him, is now Parque Amigos de José Martí.

    You can visit Ybor City Historic District, V. M. Ybor Cigar Factory (Ybor Factory Building), Parque Amigos de José Martí

    Sources: nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  12. 1892

    Hugh Macfarlane founds West Tampa

    West Tampa

    In 1892 Scottish businessman Hugh Macfarlane founded West Tampa across the Hillsborough River as a second cigar town; by 1900 it was one of the most populous places in Florida, mostly Cuban cigar workers. Together Ybor City and West Tampa made Tampa, in the National Park Service's words, the hand-rolled cigar capital of the world by 1900. Tampa annexed West Tampa in 1925.

    Sources: en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · npgallery.nps.gov (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  13. 1892

    Feb 29

    St. Petersburg incorporates as a town

    Downtown St. Petersburg

    St. Petersburg incorporated as a town on Feb. 29, 1892, and reincorporated as a city in 1903, when it still had only a few hundred residents.

    Source: stpete.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  14. 1897

    Jan 15

    Plant opens the Hotel Belleview at Belleair

    Largo & Mid-Pinellas

    Henry Plant's Hotel Belleview opened on a bluff at Belleair, south of Clearwater, overlooking Clearwater Bay; the Henry B. Plant Museum's chronology dates the opening to 1897 (Wikipedia gives Jan. 15, 1897), while the hotel's present owner says 1896. Restored as The Belleview Inn, it is, its owner says, the only Plant hotel still operating as a hotel.

    You can visit The Belleview Inn

    Sources: plantmuseum.com (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab) · opalcollection.com (opens in a new tab) · en.wikipedia.org (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026

  15. 1898

    May

    Tampa becomes the Army's port of embarkation for Cuba

    Downtown Tampa

    In 1898 the government chose Tampa as the official port of embarkation for the Spanish-American War. About 30,000 soldiers, among them Theodore Roosevelt and his Rough Riders, arrived by late spring and camped in seven army camps, and from May the Tampa Bay Hotel served as headquarters for the officers planning the campaign; the war lasted 10 weeks.

    You can visit Henry B. Plant Museum

    Sources: nps.gov (opens in a new tab) · plantmuseum.com (opens in a new tab)Checked Sep 27, 2026
