Sheet 2 · SP

St. Petersburg

St. Petersburg, the second-largest city in the Tampa Bay area, sits on the Pinellas peninsula between Tampa Bay and the Gulf of Mexico. Its downtown waterfront has the St. Pete Pier, the Dalí and other museums along a string of bayfront parks, and Central Avenue runs west through arts districts full of murals, galleries, bars and breweries. Gulfport, a small arts town on Boca Ciega Bay, is on this sheet too.

Known for the St. Pete Pier, the Dalí Museum, murals and galleries, craft breweries, the Rays at Tropicana Field and Weedon Island Preserve.

SPDowntown St. Petersburg

St. Pete · District

Downtown St. Petersburg

St. Petersburg's downtown on Tampa Bay, where Beach Drive and a string of bayfront parks lead to the 26-acre St. Pete Pier, and museums including the Dalí and the Museum of Fine Arts are within walking distance. Central Avenue runs west through the Central Arts and EDGE districts, with murals, galleries, bars and music venues such as Jannus Live.

the St. Pete Pier · the Dalí Museum · the Museum of Fine Arts · Beach Drive restaurants · murals and galleries on Central Avenue · the Saturday Morning Market

SPGrand Central & Warehouse Arts District

St. Pete · District

Grand Central & Warehouse Arts District

West of downtown, the Grand Central District runs along Central Avenue from 16th to 31st Street with galleries, shops, breweries and bars; Visit St. Pete/Clearwater calls it the city's original LGBTQ+ neighborhood and the birthplace of St. Pete Pride. Just south, the Warehouse Arts District fills former industrial buildings with artists' studios, galleries and breweries. The guide also files Tropicana Field here: the Rays' domed ballpark reopened on April 6, 2026, after repairs to roof damage from Hurricane Milton.

LGBTQ+ bars and shops on Central Avenue · Warehouse Arts District studios and galleries · the Imagine Museum · craft breweries · Tropicana Field

SPOld Northeast & Snell Isle

St. Pete · Neighborhood

Old Northeast & Snell Isle

The North Shore Historic District, known as Historic Old Northeast, is a mostly residential neighborhood next to downtown with nearly 3,000 historic buildings between 4th Street North and Tampa Bay. It holds Sunken Gardens, a botanical garden more than a century old that the city now runs, and bayfront parks; Snell Isle, developed on a mangrove island in the 1920s, and Crescent Lake are included here.

historic homes of the North Shore Historic District · Sunken Gardens · North Shore Park and its beach · Snell Isle

SPSouth St. Pete & Midtown

St. Pete · District

South St. Pete & Midtown

The south side of St. Petersburg includes Midtown and 22nd Street South, known as The Deuces, once the city's Black Main Street, where the Dr. Carter G. Woodson African American Museum and the Historic Manhattan Casino stand. Farther south, Boyd Hill Nature Preserve covers 400 acres connected to Lake Maggiore, with trails through habitat for gopher tortoises and alligators.

The Deuces on 22nd Street South · the Woodson African American Museum · the Historic Manhattan Casino · Boyd Hill Nature Preserve · Lake Maggiore

SPNortheast St. Pete & Weedon Island

St. Pete · District

Northeast St. Pete & Weedon Island

The northeast corner of St. Petersburg on Tampa Bay, where the Gandy Bridge crosses Old Tampa Bay to Tampa. Its main draw is Weedon Island Preserve, a 3,000-acre county preserve with more than 4.7 miles of boardwalks and trails, paddling trails, a fishing pier and a cultural and natural history center, on a site where Indigenous peoples lived for thousands of years. Includes Gateway and Carillon.

Weedon Island Preserve · boardwalks and paddling trails · the Weedon Island Preserve Cultural and Natural History Center · the Gandy Bridge to Tampa

SPWest St. Pete

St. Pete · District

West St. Pete

St. Petersburg's western neighborhoods, including Tyrone, around the Tyrone Square mall, Azalea and Jungle Prada, where a city park holds part of an archaeological site with Tocobaga mounds, listed on the National Register in 2003 and identified as the landing place of the Narváez expedition. freeFall Theatre stages plays and musicals on Central Avenue, and St. Petersburg College's Gibbs Campus planetarium gives free public shows on Wednesday and Friday evenings while the college is in session.

Tyrone Square mall · the Jungle Prada mound site · freeFall Theatre · free shows at the SPC Planetarium

SPGulfport

St. Pete · City

Gulfport

A small city on Boca Ciega Bay bordering St. Petersburg, with a walkable waterfront district of independent boutiques, galleries and restaurants along Beach and Shore boulevards. The Gulfport Casino, a waterfront ballroom whose current building dates from the 1930s, is on the National Register, and the Gulfport Tuesday Fresh Market runs year-round.

independent boutiques and galleries · the Gulfport Casino · the Gulfport Tuesday Fresh Market · the Gulfport Night Market · Clam Bayou Nature Preserve

SPThe Dalí Museum

St. Pete · Museum · Downtown St. Petersburg

The Dalí Museum

Museum devoted to Salvador Dalí, built on the collection of A. Reynolds and Eleanor Morse; the museum says it holds over 2,400 works in all media. Gallery admission covers the permanent collection, special exhibitions and the Avant-garden; the Dalí Dome shows immersive projections for an added fee.

4
SPMuseum of Fine Arts, St. Petersburg

St. Pete · Museum · Downtown St. Petersburg

Museum of Fine Arts, St. Petersburg

Art museum on the downtown waterfront, founded by collector Margaret Acheson Stuart and opened in 1965; the museum says it was the city's first art museum. The collection galleries are closed Sep 22–Nov 21, 2026 for a reinstallation; the New African Masquerades exhibition, shop and café stay open.

5
SPChihuly Collection

St. Pete · Museum · Downtown St. Petersburg

Chihuly Collection

Permanent collection of glass art by Dale Chihuly, run by the Morean Arts Center in a building designed for it. A ticket includes a live glassblowing demonstration at the Morean Glass Studio nearby.

8
SPSunshine Skyway Bridge

St. Pete · Landmark · South St. Pete & Midtown

Sunshine Skyway Bridge

Cable-stayed toll bridge carrying I-275 and US 19 across Lower Tampa Bay from St. Petersburg to Terra Ceia in Manatee County, about 4.1 miles long with a 1,200-foot main span; it opened on April 20, 1987. It replaced the original Skyway, part of which fell in 1980 when a freighter struck it.

6
SPThe St. Pete Pier

St. Pete · Pier · Downtown St. Petersburg

The St. Pete Pier

Pier district on Tampa Bay with seven restaurants (Pier Teak has a rooftop level), public art, the Glazer Family Playground and a splash pad, Spa Beach, a fishing deck, a weekend marketplace and the Tampa Bay Watch Discovery Center. The St. Petersburg Museum of History sits at its entrance.

7
SPMahaffey Theater

St. Pete · Performing arts · Downtown St. Petersburg

Mahaffey Theater

A 2,031-seat theater on the downtown St. Petersburg waterfront for touring Broadway, classical, pop, rock, comedy and family shows and concerts by the Florida Orchestra. It opened in 1965 as part of the Bayfront Center and took the Mahaffey name after a 1987-88 renovation.

2
SPTropicana Field

St. Pete · Arena or stadium · Grand Central & Warehouse Arts District

Tropicana Field

Domed ballpark in St. Petersburg and the home of the Tampa Bay Rays. Hurricane Milton tore off most of its roof in October 2024; the Rays played 2025 at Steinbrenner Field in Tampa and returned to the repaired Trop for the 2026 season.

1
SPTed Peters Famous Smoked Fish

St. Pete · Restaurant · West St. Pete

Ted Peters Famous Smoked Fish

Smoked-fish restaurant at its current site since 1951: fish are laid on racks and smoked over red oak for 4 to 6 hours, and the German potato salad recipe from the early years is still served. The restaurant says the family business now spans five generations.

SPVinoy Park

St. Pete · Park · Downtown St. Petersburg

Vinoy Park

An 11.6-acre city park on the downtown St. Petersburg waterfront, next to the Vinoy hotel, used for big outdoor festivals such as the Mainsail Art Festival and the Tampa Bay Blues Festival, plus concerts and triathlons.

Hours not listed · Price not listed

SPWilliams Park

St. Pete · Park · Downtown St. Petersburg

Williams Park

St. Petersburg's first park, a full city block downtown between 3rd and 4th Streets North and 1st and 2nd Avenues North, with a 1954 bandshell used for outdoor concerts at noon and on weekends.

Hours not listed · Price not listed

SPStraub Park

St. Pete · Park · Downtown St. Petersburg

Straub Park

City park along Bayshore Drive on the downtown St. Petersburg waterfront, in two parts: North Straub Park (400 Bayshore Dr. NE) and South Straub Park (198 Bayshore Dr. NE). South Straub Park hosts outdoor events such as the free St. Petersburg Fine Art Festival (Feb 6-7, 2027).

Hours not listed · Price not listed

St. Pete · Park · Old Northeast & Snell Isle

North Shore Park

A City of St. Petersburg park with a beach, open from 30 minutes before sunrise to 11 p.m.

30 minutes before sunrise to 11 p.m. · Price not listed

SPSawgrass Lake Park

St. Pete · Park · Northeast St. Pete & Weedon Island

Sawgrass Lake Park

A 400-acre park in the Lealman community run with the Southwest Florida Water Management District, with a boardwalk and nature trail through a maple swamp and an observation tower; thousands of birds pass through in fall and spring. Fishing is not allowed.

Daily, 7 a.m. to sunset; closed the day after Thanksgiving and Dec. 25. · Price not listed

SPWeedon Island Preserve

St. Pete · Nature preserve · Northeast St. Pete & Weedon Island

Weedon Island Preserve

A 3,000-acre county preserve on Tampa Bay in north St. Petersburg where Indigenous people lived for thousands of years. Its Cultural and Natural History Center has exhibits on that history, and the preserve says a 1,100-year-old dugout canoe from its shoreline can be seen in the Welcome Center.

Preserve and trails: daily, 7 a.m. to about 15 minutes before sunset. Cultural and Natural History Center: Thursday to Saturday 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., Sunday 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. · Free admission to the preserve and the Cultural and Natural History Center; donations welcome

Getting around

Getting around

Arriving

  • St. Pete-Clearwater International Airport PIEAirportThe airport on Roosevelt Blvd (SR 686), 4 miles north of St. Petersburg, served by Allegiant and Sun Country (domestic) and BermudAir (international), with nonstops to more than 60 cities. Rideshare pickup is in front of the terminal at Arrivals; taxis and shuttles wait in the Ground Transportation lot outside baggage claim; PSTA's Grouper runs to Clearwater Beach.

Transit, trolleys and boats

  • Tampa Bay Ferry (St. Petersburg-Tampa)FerryThere is no passenger ferry across the bay between downtown St. Petersburg and Tampa right now: PSTA is preparing a new Tampa Bay Ferry service to replace the former Cross-Bay Ferry, with two vessels (Bay Breeze and Clipper) being refurbished in Tarpon Springs and a permanent dock planned in downtown St. Petersburg.
  • 727 Airport Express (downtown St. Petersburg to Tampa International Airport)BusA new PSTA premium express bus between nine stops in downtown St. Petersburg and Tampa International Airport, running over the Howard Frankland Bridge express lanes on I-275. PSTA says it will run daily from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. on hybrid buses with luggage racks.
  • PSTA Route 100 Express (St. Petersburg to Tampa)Bus · Regular PSTA fare ($2.25).A weekday express bus between the St. Pete Pier area (2nd Ave NE and Beach Drive) and the Marion Transit Center in downtown Tampa, stopping at Gateway Mall and Britton Plaza.
  • PSTA busesBusPinellas County's transit agency: fixed-route buses, the SunRunner rapid bus, the Suncoast Beach Trolley along the Gulf beaches, and express routes 100 and 300 to downtown Tampa. Tap a contactless card (not American Express), Apple Pay, Google Pay or the Flamingo Fares app to get the daily and monthly fare caps.
  • SunRunnerBus rapid transitTampa Bay's first bus rapid transit line, a 10-mile, 16-stop route with dedicated bus lanes between downtown St. Petersburg and St. Pete Beach via the Grand Central and EDGE districts and South Pasadena; PSTA says the trip takes about 35 minutes. Buses have level boarding, three interior bike racks, free Wi-Fi and charging at every seat.
  • Central Avenue TrolleyTrolley · Free.A free PSTA trolley along Central Avenue between Grand Central Station and the St. Pete Pier (2nd Ave NE and Beach Drive), running every 30 minutes daily.
  • Downtown LooperTrolley · Free.A free trolley loop around downtown St. Petersburg every 15-20 minutes, stopping at the Vinoy, St. Pete City Hall, the SouthCore garage, Johns Hopkins All Children's Hospital, USF St. Petersburg, the Dali Museum (during museum hours only), the Mahaffey Theater, the James Museum, the Sundial garage and the St. Pete Pier. All trolleys have a wheelchair lift; PSTA's Central Avenue Trolley connects with it on Bayshore Drive near the Pier approach.
